# 📊 CSR & Social Sector Data Analysis

## 📌 Overview
This project analyzes **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)** initiatives across India to understand how funds and services are distributed across states, districts, and development sectors.  
The goal is to identify high-impact areas, uncover gaps, and provide actionable insights for better CSR planning.

---

## 🎯 Objective
- Analyze distribution of CSR projects across districts and sectors  
- Identify top and bottom performing regions  
- Determine sectors receiving maximum and minimum funding  
- Detect underserved districts and coverage gaps  
- Provide data-driven recommendations for better CSR allocation  

---

## ❗ Problem Statement
CSR activities are often unevenly distributed, with some regions receiving more attention while others remain underserved.  
This project aims to uncover **regional imbalance, sector dominance, and inefficiencies** in CSR fund allocation.

---

## 📂 Data Source
- **Source:** Ministry of Corporate Affairs (India)  
- **Dataset:** CSR Projects Data  
- **Timeline:** 2014 – 2020  
- **Domain:** Social Welfare / Socio-Economic Development

## 📁 Project Workflow
1. **Data Cleaning**
   - Removed duplicates  
   - Handled missing values  
   - Standardized state & district names  
   - Treated “Not Mentioned” values  

2. **Data Transformation**
   - Converted data into Fact & Dimension tables  
   - Corrected data types  

3. **Data Modeling**
   - Built **Star Schema**  
   - Established relationships between tables  

4. **DAX Calculations**
   - Total Projects  
   - Total States & Districts  
   - Total Project Outlay  
   - Amount Spent  
   - Ranking of sectors  
   - Spend Status (Low / High / Exact)

## 📈 Key Insights

### 🔸 Descriptive Insights
- CSR projects are highly concentrated in a few districts  
- Top 5 states dominate overall CSR contributions  
- Limited sectors receive majority of funding  

### 🔸 Diagnostic Insights
- CSR concentration driven by:
  - Industrial presence  
  - Ease of execution  
  - Corporate accessibility  
- Low-performing regions face:
  - Lack of awareness  
  - Limited corporate presence  
  - Weak implementation capacity  

### 🔸 Predictive Insights
- CSR funding will continue to concentrate in top regions  
- Underserved districts may remain neglected  
- Dominant sectors will keep attracting more investments  

### 🔸 Prescriptive Insights
- Redistribute CSR funds to underserved regions  
- Introduce minimum CSR allocation policies  
- Encourage investment in less-funded sectors  
- Improve data standardization and governance

## 🧾 Conclusion
The analysis reveals that CSR activities are often **concentrated and compliance-driven rather than impact-driven**.  
There is a strong need for **equitable distribution, strategic planning, and data-driven decision-making** to ensure balanced regional development.
